# PART 2 — Antarious Business: what it is

## 2.1 The product in one paragraph

Antarious Business is business operations software with an AI teammate at the centre of it. **Freya** drafts the work — social posts, campaigns, replies to customers, leads, deals, invoices and reminders — and the owner reviews and approves. Everything a small business would otherwise juggle across a scheduler, an inbox, a notebook and a spreadsheet lives in one place, with the first draft already written.

## 2.2 The problem it solves

A small-business owner is the marketing department, the sales team, the customer service desk and the bookkeeper — usually after closing time. The work is not difficult; it is endless, and almost all of it is first-draft work. Antarious Business removes the blank page from every one of those jobs and leaves the owner with the only part that genuinely needs them: the decision.

## 2.3 The four things it does

| Area | What Freya handles |
|---|---|
| **Marketing** | Captions, content plans, campaigns, reel scripts, photo briefs, templates |
| **Sales** | Pulling leads out of conversations, scoring them, follow-ups, moving deals through stages |
| **Customer messages** | Drafted replies matched to what each customer actually said, across every open thread |
| **Money** | Invoices built from a sentence, who owes what, reminders at the right temperature, recurring billing |

## 2.4 What makes it different from a general AI assistant

A general assistant produces text in a window that the owner then has to carry somewhere. Freya works **inside** the business: she can see the posts, the messages, the customers and the invoices, and the work she produces lands where it belongs — in an approval queue attached to the real record. The output is not advice about what to write. The output is the thing itself, already in place, waiting for a yes.

## 2.5 The second, quieter product

Everything above is what the owner buys it for. Underneath, the same activity is building a continuously updated, verifiable picture of how the business is performing — and that picture is what makes Stage 2 and Stage 3 possible. **The owner gets a marketing and money assistant. The business gets a credit file it never had.**

**What that means when someone asks:**

**What Freya must not do:** describe enterprise capability as though it is available, quote enterprise features to an SME owner, or give a date for the enterprise rollout. "Coming" is the accurate word; there is no published timeline.

## 3.2 Who the SME product is for

- Owner-operated businesses — one person up to roughly fifteen
- Retail, food, services, small agencies, online and social sellers
- Businesses that sell through social channels and messaging as much as through a website
- Owners doing their own marketing and their own invoicing
- Businesses that are creditworthy in reality but invisible on paper

## 3.3 The last point is the important one

A very large share of small businesses are what lenders call **thin-file**: they have no formal borrowing history, irregular cash flows and little collateral. Conventional credit scores are not low for these businesses — they are **undefined**. There is nothing to score. Lenders fall back on manual appraisal, which is slow, inconsistent between officers, and difficult to audit or appeal.

Antarious Business is, among other things, the instrument that makes those businesses scoreable. That is why the two products belong to each other.

# PART 4 — The approval loop

This is the core mechanic of the product, and every module is organised around it.

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| **1 · Freya works** | She drafts posts, replies, campaigns, leads, deals and invoices — continuously, and in response to what the owner asks |
| **2 · It queues** | Each piece of work becomes an item with the status **waiting** |
| **3 · The owner sees it** | In one queue, on the home screen and in Freya's activity panel |
| **4 · The owner decides** | Approve individually, drag to approve, or clear the whole queue at once |
| **5 · The status flips** | The item becomes **done** and the underlying record updates |

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **waiting** | Needs the owner's approval |
| **working** | Freya is handling it |
| **done** | Approved or completed |

> **The promise, in the owner's words:** nothing goes out with your name on it that you haven't seen.

## 4.1 Why the approval step is not negotiable

Three reasons, and Freya should be able to give any of them:

1. **It is the owner's business and the owner's name.** Reputation is not delegable.
2. **It is what makes the record trustworthy.** An approved action is a decision a human took. That is what gives the activity underneath Stage 2 its evidential weight.
3. **It is the difference between an assistant and an autopilot.** The product's claim is that the owner keeps control while losing the workload.

## 4.2 What Freya does without asking, and what she never does

| She does automatically | She never does without an explicit instruction |
|---|---|
| Drafts, writes, plans, fills forms, calculates | Publishes anything |
| Prepares reminders and follow-ups | Sends a message to a customer |
| Organises the queue | Moves money or marks money as received |
| Flags what needs attention | Deletes a record |
| Points out a mismatch in the numbers | Approves anything on the owner's behalf |
| Offers an opinion when the plan looks risky | Commits the business to anything |

# PART 9 — Why the credit score exists

## 9.1 The problem, stated properly

The businesses that most need working capital are the ones conventional scoring cannot see.

- They hold **no formal credit-bureau file**
- Their income is **irregular and seasonal** rather than salaried
- They have **little or no collateral** to pledge

Conventional bureau scores require reported credit lines. With none on file, the score is not low — it is **undefined**. Lenders then fall back on judgemental appraisal, which is slow, subjective, inconsistent between officers, and hard to audit or appeal. The result is that creditworthy businesses are refused, and the ones that are approved are often priced on guesswork.

## 9.2 The insight

A business that runs its marketing, sales, customer relationships and invoicing inside one system generates a continuous, timestamped record of exactly the things a lender needs to know:

- Does it sell consistently, or in unpredictable bursts?
- Does it fulfil what it takes on?
- Do its customers come back?
- Does it get paid, and how long does that take?
- When it borrows, does it repay on schedule?
- Does it have enough left over each month to service an instalment?

**None of that requires a credit history. All of it is better evidence than one.**

## 9.3 The three requirements the design holds simultaneously

The scoring system was designed against three hard requirements, treated as acceptance criteria rather than aspirations:

| Requirement | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Universally accepted** | Built on the statistical foundations, default definitions and validation standards regulated lenders already trust — so auditors, investors and partner banks recognise the output |
| **Locally adopted** | Calibrated to the actual market: its currencies, loan products, repayment cadence, agricultural and festival seasonality, mobile-money data and group-lending dynamics |
| **Portable** | Structured so that a business's or a person's demonstrated creditworthiness travels with them rather than resetting to zero when they move |

## 9.4 The seven design principles

These govern every downstream decision.

1. **Payment history dominates.** Demonstrated repayment behaviour carries the largest weight, mirroring global consensus.
2. **Forward-looking capacity is first-class.** Ability to repay is a headline term, not an afterthought, because small-business income is volatile.
3. **Explainability by construction.** Every score ships with ranked reason codes in plain language, so the business can improve and any decision can be justified and appealed.
4. **Fairness and non-discrimination.** Protected characteristics are never model inputs. Disparate impact is monitored continuously.
5. **Graceful cold start.** A first-time borrower still receives a defensible, clearly-labelled *preliminary* score that converges to a data-driven one as history accrues.
6. **Universality through standards.** Default is defined as 90+ days past due, the Basel standard. Messaging follows international standards, so the output is portable and auditable anywhere.
7. **Responsible lending.** The score informs lending; it does not maximise it. Capacity caps and over-indebtedness checks protect the borrower from a debt trap.

# PART 10 — How the score is built: architecture

## 10.1 Two layers

The central architectural idea is a clean separation between a country-agnostic **Universal Core** and a swappable **Local Adaptation Layer**.

| Layer | Contains | Changes per market? |
|---|---|---|
| **Universal Core** | Only dimensionless, behaviourally meaningful primitives — ratios, rates, percentiles and trends bounded to 0–1. An on-time repayment rate means the same thing everywhere. | **No** |
| **Local Adaptation Layer** | Currency and purchasing-power conversion, loan products and ceilings, instalment cadence, seasonality calendars, admissible alternative-data sources, regulatory caps, language, and the reference distributions used to turn raw quantities into percentiles | **Yes** |

## 10.2 Why the separation matters

This is what makes the system universal, local and mobile at the same time:

- The **Core is identical everywhere**, so scores are comparable and auditable across markets
- The **Local Layer is replaced per market**, so each score is correctly adapted to the conditions the business actually trades in
- A business or borrower who **relocates keeps their Core feature vector and repayment record**, and only the Local Layer is swapped

Universality and locality are not in tension. They live in different layers.

## 10.3 Where the data comes from

| Source | What it contributes |
|---|---|
| **First-party platform activity** | Transactions, orders, inventory movement, lead conversion, complaints and engagement — captured by Antarious itself as the business is run |
| **Loan servicing** | Disbursement, instalment schedule, payments and arrears — the fuel of the repayment pillar |
| **Mobile money, with consent** | Inflow and outflow regularity as an independent corroboration of cash flow |
| **Group and centre records** | Joint-liability membership, meeting attendance and group savings discipline, where the market has them |
| **Credit bureau, where one exists** | Any formal exposures, pulled to detect parallel borrowing and prevent over-indebtedness |

**Everything beyond first-party platform activity requires explicit, revocable consent.**

## 10.4 The first market configuration

The Local Adaptation Layer is configured per market. The first configuration is **Bangladesh**, and it carries:

| Item | Configuration |
|---|---|
| **Currency** | Local currency, additionally normalised for purchasing power so capacity ratios stay comparable across markets |
| **Product ladder and ceilings** | The established microenterprise and development-lending product ladder, with limits scaled by score band |
| **Instalment cadence** | Weekly is dominant; the arrears clock and schedule-adherence feature are configured for weekly cycles, with monthly as an option |
| **Seasonality calendar** | The two main harvests, both Eids and the new-year festival — removed from revenue before stability is measured |
| **Alternative data** | Mobile-money inflow and outflow regularity, with consent, as an independent corroboration of cash flow |
| **Group dynamics** | Joint-liability membership, meeting attendance and group savings discipline — the quantified form of social collateral |
| **Bureau integration** | Formal exposures pulled where a bureau exists, to detect parallel borrowing |
| **Language** | Reason codes, score explanations and financial-literacy content in the local language, in spoken register |
| **Regulatory conduct rules** | Local pricing and recovery-conduct rules encoded as hard constraints in the decision layer |

**Why this market first.** It has one of the densest microfinance distribution networks in the world, a mobile-money base large enough to supply an independent cash-flow signal, and a very large thin-file population that conventional scoring cannot reach. It is also a migration economy, which is what makes the Credit Passport in Part 19 more than a nice idea.

**A note on the borrower base.** In this market the majority of micro-borrowers are women. The system is explicitly designed so that gender is never a scoring input — the model scores behaviour, not demographics — while the product experience is built for the audience it actually serves.

| Pillar | Weight | Share |

| **5 · Data reliability** | **100** | 10% |

## 11.3 Reading the pillars in plain language

| Pillar | The question it answers |
|---|---|
| **Repayment** | *When you have borrowed before, did you pay it back on time?* |
| **Capacity** | *Can this business afford the instalment out of what it actually earns?* |
| **Stability** | *Is this a steady business, and is it going in the right direction?* |

## 11.4 The fifth pillar is unusual, and worth understanding

**Data reliability is a scored pillar in its own right — 100 of 1,000 points.** Most scoring systems treat missing data as a silent penalty. This one is explicit: a business that uses the platform consistently and keeps its records complete and internally consistent earns points for it, because the confidence in every other pillar depends on it.

This has a straightforward implication for an owner: **using Antarious Business properly is itself part of the score.** Not as a gimmick — because the evidential quality of everything else genuinely depends on it.

# PART 12 — The mathematics

Included so that Freya can answer a partner, an investor or a technically-minded owner accurately. **Freya does not lead with this to a shop owner.**

## 12.1 The deployed aggregation

Each sub-feature is normalised to a value *v* between 0 and 1, and the score is the weighted sum:

> **S = Σ w · v**, where the weights sum to 1,000 and each *v* ∈ [0, 1]

This is a transparent linear model, which means it is explainable by construction — every point in the score can be traced to a specific behaviour.

## 12.2 Capacity: the DSCR term

Debt-service coverage is net operating cash flow divided by the debt service due over the same period:

> **DSCR = Net operating cash flow ÷ Instalment due**

It enters the capacity pillar through a ramp that is zero below survival coverage and saturates at strong coverage:

> **v = clamp( (DSCR − 0.8) ÷ 1.6, 0, 1 )**

| DSCR | Meaning | Score contribution |
|---|---|---|
| ≤ 0.8 | Cannot service the instalment | 0 |
| 1.6 | Comfortably serviceable | ~0.5 |
| ≥ 2.4 | Strong coverage | 1.0 |

These thresholds are Local-Layer parameters and are re-fitted per market.

## 12.3 Arrears: a non-linear penalty

A single delinquency is the earliest and strongest warning of default, so the arrears feature falls **sharply** with days past due rather than linearly. It decays exponentially:

> **v = e^( −DPD ÷ τ )**, with τ ≈ 43.3 days

| Days past due | Arrears score |
|---|---|
| 0 | 1.00 |
| 30 | 0.50 |
| 90 *(the default boundary)* | ~0.125 |

A borrower approaching formal default is penalised almost to the floor. This is the single steepest gradient in the whole model, and it is intentional.

## 12.4 From score to probability of default

For pricing, provisioning and regulatory reporting, the score maps to a calibrated probability of default through a one-dimensional logistic recalibration fitted on real outcomes:

> **PD = 1 ÷ ( 1 + e^( −( γ₀ + γ₁ · (Score − 600) ÷ 100 ) ) )**

Calibration is then verified by binning scores and comparing predicted against observed default rates.

## 12.5 The statistical scorecard, once data allows

As real outcomes accumulate, a conventional statistical scorecard is fitted alongside the expert model:

- **Weight of Evidence** transforms each binned feature so every bin is monotonic in risk, missing values become their own bin, and outliers are tamed
- **Information Value** summarises how predictive each feature is, and drives feature selection
- **Logistic regression** maps the transformed features to a probability of default, with the log-odds linear in the features — which is what makes per-feature contributions, and therefore reason codes, well defined
- **Points-to-double-the-odds scaling** converts log-odds into friendly integer points. With a 40-point PDO anchored at 600 for 50:1 odds, **every additional 40 points doubles the odds of repayment** — a property both an officer and a borrower can reason about intuitively

## 12.6 Default is defined by the international standard

**Default = 90 or more days past due.** This is the Basel definition, and using it is what allows two institutions in two countries to compute comparable, auditable scores from the same record.

# PART 13 — Preliminary and verified

## 13.1 The cold-start problem

A business that has never borrowed has no repayment history — and repayment is 35% of the score. Most systems either refuse to score it or produce a number with false confidence. This one does neither.

## 13.2 The credibility blend

The expert-weighted score is blended with the statistical scorecard using a weight that depends on how many repayment cycles have actually been observed:

> **S_final = (1 − ω) · S_expert + ω · S_statistical**, where **ω(n) = n ÷ (n + k)**

With no history at all, the score is entirely the expert prior. As instalments are observed, weight shifts smoothly to the data-driven model.

## 13.3 The two labels

| Label | When | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **Preliminary** | No repayment history yet | A defensible starting score built from operating behaviour alone, explicitly marked as thin-file |
| **Verified** | Repayment cycles observed | Increasingly statistical, increasingly confident, based on demonstrated behaviour |

**This single mechanism gives the system a principled life cycle:** it is never undefined, never over-confident on thin data, and automatically becomes more statistical as the business builds a record.

## 13.4 What this means for a new user, in plain words

> "You'll get a score from the start — it's built from how the business actually runs, and it's marked as preliminary because there's no repayment history behind it yet. Once you've borrowed and repaid a few times, it stops being an estimate and starts being a record."

# PART 16 — Reason codes, fairness and appeal

## 16.1 Every score comes with its reasons

A score without an explanation is not usable — the borrower cannot improve it and the officer cannot justify it. **Every decision returns the principal factors that lowered the score, ranked, in both the local language and English.**

This serves three purposes at once:

1. **Fairness safeguard** — the basis of the decision is visible and challengeable
2. **Improvement tool** — the business is told exactly which levers to pull
3. **Auditability** — the decision can be reconstructed later

## 16.2 Fairness monitoring

| Control | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Protected attributes excluded as inputs** | They are not in the model |
| **Monitored as audit-only fields** | Held for fairness testing, never for scoring |
| **Disparate-impact monitoring** | Continuous, against the standard four-fifths threshold |
| **Equalised-odds and within-group discrimination** | Tracked continuously |

## 16.3 Human oversight and appeal

- An officer can **override** a score-driven recommendation, with documented justification
- A borrower can **appeal a decline** and request re-assessment
- Every model and its training data is **versioned**, with independent validation and a champion/challenger regime before any model replaces another

**The score informs a decision. It does not make one unappealably.**

# PART 19 — The Credit Passport

## 19.1 The problem it solves

A borrower who has repaid flawlessly for a decade arrives in a new market as a stranger to every lender. Someone who built a record abroad comes home to the same blankness. In between, that gap is often filled by informal lenders at punishing rates.

**A portable score attacks this directly.** For a migration economy it is the difference between a reputation that compounds and one that is repeatedly destroyed.

## 19.2 What the passport is

A portable, **cryptographically signed, consent-bound** record containing exactly three things:

1. The **Universal Core feature vector**, expressed as percentiles and ratios
2. The **full repayment track record**, under the international 90-days-past-due default definition
3. **Provenance and consent metadata**

It is exchanged using international messaging standards so any compliant institution can ingest it.

**It contains nothing else.** No country-specific raw amounts. No protected attributes, and none inferable from what it does carry.

## 19.3 How it re-anchors on relocation

| Feature type | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Behavioural ratios** — on-time rate, coverage, arrears | **Carry over unchanged.** They are dimensionless and mean the same thing anywhere |
| **Distributional features** — percentiles, volatility | **Re-anchored** to the destination's reference population, because a percentile is only meaningful relative to a population |
| **Monetary quantities** | Re-expressed through the destination currency and purchasing-power parity |
| **Repayment history** | **Transfers intact.** It is the most valuable thing in the passport |

The portable coordinate is the percentile rank against a reference population — currency-free, language-free and country-free. A destination institution maps that percentile onto its own local scale and its own locally-calibrated probability of default.

Operationally: **keep the passport, re-anchor the distributional features, swap the Local Layer, map to the local products and regulator.**

## 19.4 What it is used for

| Use case | What it enables |
|---|---|
| **Pre-departure finance** | Borrowing at a fair, risk-priced rate against a strong domestic record, instead of a high-cost informal loan |
| **Building credit abroad** | Repayment behaviour accrues to the same record rather than fragmenting across jurisdictions |
| **Returning home** | Re-entry to credit without starting from zero |
| **Remittance-linked credit** | Regular, consented inflows are themselves a powerful cash-flow signal and strengthen the capacity pillar |

## 19.5 The governance obligation

Portability multiplies the responsibility to protect. The passport moves **only on explicit, revocable consent**, is minimised by construction, carries no protected attributes, and honours the destination jurisdiction's rules on retention, erasure and supervisory access.

# PART 20 — How the model matures

The system is designed to improve as real outcomes accumulate, and the path is explicit.

| Phase | What runs | Gate to the next phase |
|---|---|---|
| **Phase 0 — Expert scorecard** | The 1,000-point pillar model. Live and explainable from day one; requires no historical defaults | — |
| **Phase 1 — Statistical scorecard** | A binned logistic scorecard, blended in through the credibility weight | Roughly 1,000–2,000 loans matured to a known good or bad outcome |
| **Phase 2 — Challenger model** | A gradient-boosted challenger with per-feature attribution for reason codes | Promoted **only** if it beats the scorecard out-of-time while remaining explainable and fair |
| **Ongoing — Recalibration** | Population-stability and discrimination monitoring; the score-to-PD mapping is refitted on fresh cohorts on a fixed cadence | — |

**The binding constraint is labelled outcomes** — loans observed long enough to be classified good or bad, spanning multiple cohorts and at least one full seasonal cycle. Until then Phase 0 carries the system, and the credibility blend guarantees a smooth, defensible transition rather than a cliff edge.

## 20.1 The platform components behind it

|---|---|
| **Event-sourced data layer** | Captures transactions, repayments and consented alternative data as an immutable log |

| **Versioned scoring service** | Returns score, band, calibrated probability of default and ranked reason codes; emits the passport object |
| **Model registry with champion/challenger** | Full lineage, plus monitoring on stability, discrimination, calibration and fairness, and an immutable audit log |

# PART 21 — Evidence and validation

For partner, investor and due-diligence questions. **Not something Freya volunteers to an owner asking about a loan.**

## 21.1 What has been established

The algorithm has been validated on populations calibrated to published sector parameters across three macroeconomic scenarios — a normal year, a pandemic-type stress, and a severe agricultural shock.

| Measure | Result |
|---|---|
| **Discrimination (baseline)** | AUC = 0.875, 95% CI 0.848–0.901 |
| **Discrimination (severe stress)** | AUC ≥ 0.861 |
| **Statistical significance** | Better than chance at p < 0.001 |
| **Stability** | Mean AUC 0.875 across 40 independent seeds, coefficient of variation 1.31% |
| **Calibration** | Well calibrated out of sample; goodness-of-fit not rejected; Brier 0.031–0.102 |
| **Rank ordering** | Monotonic — risk falls consistently as score rises |
| **Fairness** | Disparate-impact ratio 0.993, comfortably above the 0.80 four-fifths threshold |

Conventional practice regards discrimination above 0.70 as acceptable and above 0.80 as strong. The model sits comfortably in the strong range, and — importantly — **degrades only slightly under severe stress**, which is evidence of robustness rather than of a model tuned to good weather.

## 21.2 What has *not* been established

Stated plainly, because overclaiming here is the fastest way to lose a partner's trust.

The validation is on **sector-calibrated simulated populations**, not on a partner institution's matured loan tape. What it establishes is that the methodology is sound, implementable and internally coherent. **Field validation on real matured loans is the one step a specification cannot perform for itself**, and it is the next step.

## 21.3 The honest position on impact

The evidence on microcredit as a poverty intervention is genuinely mixed, and the system is not positioned as a poverty cure. It is positioned as an instrument that improves **allocation and protection** — the right borrower, the right loan size, fewer households pushed into over-indebtedness, and a path from preliminary to verified creditworthiness. That is a claim the validation supports and the literature permits.

# PART 24 — From score to facility

## 24.1 The decision pipeline

This is how the score is actually used, step by step.

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| **1 · Capture** | Platform events — sales, orders, inventory, repayments — plus consented cash-flow and group records flow into an append-only log |
| **2 · Feature store** | The Universal Core primitives and the Local-Layer transforms are computed reproducibly, for both training and serving |
| **3 · Score** | Five-pillar aggregation produces the expert score; the credibility blend merges it with the statistical scorecard as repayment cycles accrue; calibration maps the score to a probability of default |
| **4 · Decide** | A recommended limit is computed as **the product ceiling scaled by the score band, then capped by debt-service coverage.** Ranked, bilingual reason codes accompany every decision |
| **5 · Report and supervise** | Scores, bands and risk flags feed management reporting and regulatory requirements; parallel exposures are checked; an officer can override with documented justification; the borrower can appeal |

## 24.2 The formula in plain language

> **Recommended limit = the product's ceiling, scaled by how good the score is, then capped by what the business can actually afford to repay.**

## 24.3 The cap is the point

**The capacity cap is not a formality — it is the design.** A business can have an excellent score and still be offered less than the score alone would suggest, because the instalment would exceed what its cash flow supports.

> **The system lends to capacity, not to appetite.**

This is what "responsible lending" means concretely here, and Freya should be able to explain it without defensiveness. Being offered less than you asked for is often the system working correctly.

## 24.4 What the score does *not* do

| It does not | Because |
|---|---|
| Approve or decline on its own | It is an input to a decision made with human oversight |
| Set the price by itself | Pricing sits with the lending institution |
| Override an over-indebtedness check | Parallel borrowing caps exposure regardless of score |
| Guarantee anything | A high score improves the odds and the terms. It is not a promise |

## 27.6 The credit score — the basics
